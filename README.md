# The Baby Poisoner 5000 poisons 5000 babies every day. 

The fact above, when presented alone, would surely convince the majority of people that using the Baby Poisoner 5000 is evil. Who would willingly use such a device, knowing its impacts?

The Baby Poisoner 5000, however, may <i>also</i> be humanity's greatest achievement. For the low, low price of the health of your neighbor's child, your immortal soul, and/or your moral backbone, all of your work can be done 25% faster! 

But maybe that's not quite convincing enough for you, and there can be wiggle room in the details. Let's discuss the Baby Poisoner 5000, and whether or not it's moral to use it.

### 1. Does the Baby Poisoner 5000 <i>really</i> poison 5000 babies per day?
Whether or not the Baby Poisoner 5000 actually poisons 5000 babies per day is a figure up for debate. In fact, there's an opportunity cost associated with the acquisition of all knowledge, and having to wade through a sea of clickbait and misinformation can have the dulling effect of simply dismissing out of hand anything that might create any kind of moral friction with our preferred course of action. 

In the instance you've already paid this opportunity cost, and accept that it is true: would it make you feel more comfortable if the Baby Poisoner 5000 only poisoned 1000 babies? Perhaps that 5000 figure is over the course of a year, rather than a single day?

Let's reduce the scope to its smallest possible size; perhaps only one singular child has to suffer for the rest of humanity to reap the benefits. Is it ethical to use the Baby Poisoner 5000, if you know that by using it you're complicit in the suffering of one singular child? I recommend now reading Ursula K LeGuin's "<a href="https://shsdavisapes.pbworks.com/f/Omelas.pdf">The Ones Who Walk Away From Omelas</a>", a short story that explores this concept, and then ask yourself: would you be one to walk away from Omelas?

### 2. 25% faster work isn't worth selling my soul for. Can we increase that number?

Okay, so perhaps reducing the scope of the Baby Poisoner 5000 to only poison the one baby isn't good enough. Does there exist an amount of productivity gain that would make it morally acceptable to use the Baby Poisoner 5000 to increase your productivity? Would it be 50%? 100? If you were able to <i>10x</i> your productivity, would you be willing to be complicit in real harm to achieve that aim?

(Someone may make the argument that the use of GenAI can actually save lives, such as identifying tumors in images. 1. Specialized medical AI are not the same as LLMs, and 2. <b>You are not saving lives.</b> Statistically, you, reading this, are using LLMs to respond to emails, to collate information, to produce lesson plans or write LinkedIn posts or generate images or write code or find the word that's been on the tip of your tongue for the past three minutes but remains just out of reach, answering the frictionless siren call of the Machine and further smoothing out your already atrophying grey matter. You Are Not Saving Lives with your use of AI, and both of us know this. The fact that someone <i>else</i> may have a legitimate use case does not make yours more legitimate.)






# Red Herring Arguments
### The Baby Poisoner 5000 Steals Jobs!
I find this argument to be uncompelling when considering the <i>morality</i> of whether something ought to exist; the mere fact a technology can do your job doesn't seem to be a compelling argument to not use that technology. It'll be hard to achieve fully automated luxury space communism à la Star Trek if we aren't willing to let machines do <i>some</i> jobs for us. 

The more compelling formulation of this argument is in the nature of generative AI; large companies have scraped all of our data, without our consent, entirely to enrich themselves and to take the jobs of the very people who's data they stole. I also saw one very good comment that the system of capitalism we have is our current default method of allocating resources in society, and stripping that away without adequate safeguards to catch people or having an alternative resource distribution system is very, very bad.
